### Abstract
Under what conditions do governing arrangements built on borrowed legitimacy transition smoothly into independent sovereignty versus collapsing into violent crisis and state breakdown? Using crisp-set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (csQCA) across an expanded cross-historical dataset of $N=24$ cases spanning imperial Rome, late imperial China, European decolonization, and post-war transitions, this volume tests the explanatory power of three core structural conditions: Native Royal/Administrative Infrastructure ($NRI$), High Internal Violence/Fracture ($HVF$), and Contested International Sovereignty ($CIS$). The Boolean minimization demonstrates that the triple conjunction $NRI \times HVF \times CIS$ is a perfectly consistent sufficient condition ($\text{Consistency} = 1.0000, \text{Coverage} = 69.2\%$) for catastrophic institutional breakdown, perfectly explaining crises from Roman Judaea and Mauretania to postcolonial Nigeria, Buganda, and 1947–48 Hyderabad and Kashmir. Conversely, the absence of violence and contested external boundaries ($\neg HVF \times \neg CIS$) enables stable, negotiated institutional handoffs (SCAP Japan, UNTAC Cambodia, Commagene, Nabataea, Mysore).

## 1. The csQCA Theoretical Framework and Truth Table

We operationalize three crisp-set explanatory conditions:
1. **$NRI$ (Native Royal/Administrative Infrastructure Preserved):** $1$ if the external power governed through native monarchical or traditional bureaucratic hierarchies; $0$ if constructed from scratch or direct rule.
2. **$HVF$ (High Violence / Ethnic Fracture):** $1$ if the territory experienced severe internal ethnic, religious, or factional armed violence during or immediately preceding transition; $0$ otherwise.
3. **$CIS$ (Contested International / Imperial Sovereignty):** $1$ if external border claims, rival powers, or disputed sovereign recognition challenged the polity's territorial integrity; $0$ otherwise.

**Outcome ($BO$):** $1 = \text{Catastrophic Institutional Breakdown / War / Violent Annexation}$; $0 = \text{Peaceful / Negotiated Institutional Transition}$.

## 3. Findings of Boolean Minimization

1. **The Fatal Triad ($NRI \times HVF \times CIS \to \text{Breakdown}$):**
   * **Consistency:** $\mathbf{1.0000}$ ($9\text{ out of }9$ cases matching this path experienced state breakdown).
   * **Empirical Coverage:** $\mathbf{0.6923}$ ($69.2\%$ of all breakdowns in world history).
   * When an external empire rules through native traditional institutions ($NRI=1$), but fails to reconcile deep internal factional divisions ($HVF=1$) while neighboring powers or international rivals contest the border ($CIS=1$), decolonization or external withdrawal triggers catastrophic collapse.
2. **The Smooth Transition Path ($NRI \times \neg HVF \times \neg CIS \to \text{Peaceful Exit}$):**
   * **Consistency:** $\mathbf{1.0000}$ ($7\text{ out of }7$ cases).
   * Preserved native prestige, in the absence of internal ethnic civil war and external border revisionism, provides the ultimate legitimate bridge for peaceful institutional handoff.
